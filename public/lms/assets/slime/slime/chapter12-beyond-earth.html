Big Idea

A Universe Bigger Than We Imagined

Look up at night — you see the Moon, planets, stars. Each is incredibly far away.

Earth is just one small planet around one ordinary star (the Sun). The Sun is one of BILLIONS of stars in our galaxy. And the universe has BILLIONS of galaxies!

In Ladakh, with no light pollution, you can see ~3000 stars with naked eye. But the universe has 10²² stars!
12.1

The Sun — Our Closest Star

Yes! Our Sun is a star — just very close to us.

  • Distance from Earth: ~150 million km
  • Light from Sun takes 8 minutes to reach us
  • Sun gives us heat, light, energy
  • It\'s a giant ball of HOT gases
Other stars look smaller because they\'re much, MUCH farther away.
12.2

The Moon — Our Companion

The Moon goes around Earth — about once every 29 days.

The Moon doesn\'t have its OWN light. It REFLECTS sunlight.

  • Distance: ~384,000 km from Earth
  • About ¼ the size of Earth
  • No air, no water, no life
  • Has craters (made by meteor impacts)
Phases

Phases of the Moon

The Moon looks different on different nights — it\'s the same Moon, but we see different parts lit up by the Sun.

  • 🌑 New Moon — dark side facing us
  • 🌒 Crescent (growing)
  • 🌓 Half Moon
  • 🌔 Gibbous (almost full)
  • 🌕 Full Moon
  • 🌖🌗🌘 Same in reverse
Indian months follow Moon phases — Purnima (full) and Amavasya (new).
12.3

Stars — Distant Suns

Every star you see at night is a sun like ours — just much further away.

Why don\'t we see stars during the day? The Sun\'s light overwhelms them.

  • Closest star (other than Sun): Proxima Centauri — 4.2 light-years away!
  • Light from it takes 4.2 YEARS to reach Earth
  • Stars TWINKLE because of Earth\'s atmosphere bending their light
12.4

Constellations — Star Patterns

People long ago "connected the dots" of bright stars into shapes — called constellations.

  • Ursa Major (Saptarishi / Big Dipper) — 7 bright stars
  • Orion (Mriga / the Hunter) — easy to spot in winter
  • Polaris (Dhruva tara) — the North Star — doesn\'t seem to move
Sailors used constellations for navigation for thousands of years!

Sky riders

More Things Up There

  • 🪨 Asteroids — small rocky bodies, mostly between Mars & Jupiter
  • ☄️ Comets — icy bodies with long tails
  • 🌠 Meteors / Shooting stars — small rocks burning in atmosphere
  • 🛰️ Satellites — natural (Moon) or artificial (made by humans)
India

🇮🇳 India in Space

ISRO (Indian Space Research Organisation) has done amazing things:

  • Aryabhata — India\'s first satellite (1975)
  • Chandrayaan-1 (2008) — found water on Moon!
  • Mangalyaan (2014) — to Mars on first try, cheapest Mars mission ever
  • Chandrayaan-3 (2023) — landed near Moon\'s south pole, first country to do so
  • Aditya-L1 (2023) — observing the Sun
India2

Ancient Indian Astronomy

Indians studied the sky thousands of years ago:

  • Aryabhata (476 CE) — calculated Earth\'s rotation, eclipses
  • Varahamihira — astrology + astronomy
  • Bhaskaracharya — speed of Earth, gravity
  • 27 nakshatras (star groups) in Hindu calendar
Astronauts

Indians in Space

  • Rakesh Sharma — first Indian in space (1984)
  • Kalpana Chawla — Indian-born NASA astronaut
  • Sunita Williams — Indian-American NASA astronaut, holds record for longest spacewalk by woman
  • Shubhanshu Shukla — Indian Air Force pilot, ISS mission 2025
Solar Eclipse

Eclipses

Solar eclipse: Moon comes between Sun and Earth, blocks sunlight briefly.

Lunar eclipse: Earth comes between Sun and Moon — Earth\'s shadow falls on Moon.

⚠️ NEVER look directly at the Sun, especially during a solar eclipse — it can blind you! Use special filters or a pinhole projector.

📖 Swara, Ahana & Amma — A Starry Night

Power cut. The girls grab a blanket and head to the terrace with Ahana\'s amma. No streetlights. The sky is bright with stars.

Swara: Wow, so many stars! How many are there?

Amma: About 3,000 visible by naked eye. But there are BILLIONS in our galaxy. And billions of galaxies in the universe!

Ahana: What are stars really?

Amma: They\'re like our Sun — giant balls of hot gas, making their own light. The Sun is the closest star. Others are SO far, their light takes years to reach us!

Amma points to a bright moon, then to a group of stars shaped like a pot.

Amma: See that? Saptarishi — the Big Dipper. A constellation. Indians have used star patterns for navigation and calendars for thousands of years.

Swara: What\'s the Moon then?

Amma: The Moon is Earth\'s satellite. It goes around us. It doesn\'t make its own light — it REFLECTS the Sun\'s. Every 29 days it cycles through phases — full, half, crescent, new.

Ahana: And those bright dots that don\'t twinkle?

Amma: Planets! Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn — sometimes visible. We have 8 planets orbiting the Sun. Earth is #3.

Swara: What\'s ISRO?

Amma: India\'s Space Research Organisation. We sent Chandrayaan-3 to the Moon\'s south pole in 2023 — first country to land there! Mangalyaan went to Mars. We\'re a space-faring nation!

Ahana: Were any Indians in space?

Amma: Yes! Rakesh Sharma in 1984. Then Kalpana Chawla, Sunita Williams (both Indian-origin NASA astronauts). And recently, Shubhanshu Shukla is preparing for ISS.

A streak of light flashes across the sky.

Swara: Shooting star!

Amma: Actually a meteor — a small space rock burning up in Earth\'s atmosphere. Not actually a star.

🧪 Lab Experiments

Activity 12.1 — Moon Phase Diary Easy

Procedure

  1. For one month, look at the Moon every evening.
  2. Sketch its shape.
  3. Note the date.
  4. End of month: you\'ll have all the phases!

Activity 12.2 — Find a Constellation Easy

Procedure

  1. On a clear night (away from city lights), look NORTH.
  2. Find the "saucepan" shape of Saptarishi.
  3. Trace from the two end stars — they point to Polaris (Dhruva tara).
  4. Look SOUTH in winter for Orion / Mriga.

Activity 12.3 — Build a Solar System Model Easy

Materials

  • Cardboard / chart paper
  • Coloured balls (different sizes for planets)
  • String & thumbtacks
  • Hanging frame

Procedure

  1. Place a yellow ball (Sun) at centre.
  2. String 8 balls in order around it.
  3. Different sizes & colours.
  4. Hang from ceiling — your own galaxy!

Activity 12.4 — Track an ISRO Mission Easy

Procedure

  1. Visit isro.gov.in.
  2. Find a current mission.
  3. Make a poster: when, where, what it studies.
  4. Present to class.
