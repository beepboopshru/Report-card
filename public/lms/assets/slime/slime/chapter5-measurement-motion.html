Measurement of Length and Motion

Key Concepts

"Ancient India measured in angula (finger), dhanusa (bow), and yojana (distance). Each had a clear definition. Standard units help us all speak the same language of measurement."
— Indian heritage
Big Idea

Why Measure?

To communicate ABOUT physical things — lengths, distances, sizes — we need numbers AND units. "13 handspans" means nothing unless we know whose handspans!

A measurement has two parts: a NUMBER + a UNIT.
5.1

Body Units Disagree

Deepa\'s class measured a table:

  • Anish: 13+ handspans
  • Padma: 13 handspans
  • Tasneem: ~13
  • Deepa: 13–14
  • Hardeep: 14

Why? Different sized hands! That\'s why we need STANDARD units.

India

Ancient Indian Units

  • Angula — finger width
  • Hasta — hand-length
  • Dhanusa — bow-length
  • Yojana — long distance unit
Indian craftsmen still use angula! Ruled markings have been excavated from Harappan sites — among the oldest measurement tools.
5.2

SI Units of Length

International System of Units (SI):

UnitSymbolFor
KilometrekmBig distances
MetremEveryday lengths
CentimetrecmSmall objects
MillimetremmTiny details

1 km = 1000 m | 1 m = 100 cm | 1 cm = 10 mm

Inches?

Inches & Feet

Older units still used in some places:

  • 1 inch = 2.54 cm
  • 1 foot = 12 inches = 30.48 cm
SI units are used in science worldwide.
5.3

Correct Way to Measure

  1. Place scale ALONG the object, touching it
  2. Look at the marking with your eye directly above the point — not at an angle
  3. If the 0 mark is broken, use any other clear mark and SUBTRACT
  4. Don\'t forget the UNIT!
Curved lines

Measuring Curves

Can\'t use a straight scale on a curved path. Use a thread:

  1. Lay thread along the curve
  2. Mark its end with a pen
  3. Now straighten the thread on a scale
  4. Read the length!
5.4

Motion = Change of Position

If an object\'s position keeps changing with time — it\'s in motion.

If position stays the same — at rest.

Rest and motion are RELATIVE. A passenger in a moving bus is at rest relative to the seat, but in motion relative to the road!
Types

Types of Motion

TypeExample
📏 Linear (straight line)Car on a straight road, march parade
🔄 CircularStone tied to thread, fan blades, earth around sun
🎢 Oscillatory / VibratoryPendulum, swing, guitar string
🔁 PeriodicHands of a clock, Earth\'s rotation
Combined

Some Motions Mix

A car wheel: the centre moves linearly along the road, but the wheel ITSELF rotates (circular). That\'s a COMBINATION!

  • Rolling football
  • Bus wheels
  • Earth — linear-ish around sun + rotation
Care

For Visually-Challenged Friends

Specially-made scales have raised markings — can be felt by touch. Equal access to measurement!

Unit notation

Writing Units Correctly

  • Unit names are lowercase (metre, centimetre) — EXCEPT proper nouns (kelvin = small k for unit)
  • Symbols too: km, m, cm, mm — no full stop, no plural "s"
  • Always a SPACE between number and unit: 5 cm (NOT 5cm)
Wrong: 5cms. Right: 5 cm.

📖 Swara & Ahana Measure Things

Sunday at Ahana\'s home. The girls want to compare the lengths of their pencils, their books, and Ahana\'s desk.

Swara: Let\'s use our fingers! My pencil is... 6 fingers!

Ahana: Mine is 7 fingers!

They lay the pencils side-by-side. Both pencils are EXACTLY the same length!

Swara: But how — 6 fingers ≠ 7 fingers!

Ahana\'s Amma: Your fingers and Ahana\'s fingers are different sizes! That\'s why people invented STANDARD units.

Amma: The world uses the SI units — metre (m), centimetre (cm), millimetre (mm), kilometre (km).

Ahana: So 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm. Got it!

Amma brings out a 15-cm ruler. The girls measure their pencils properly.

Amma: Eyes directly above the markings. Scale touching the pencil. Read carefully.

Swara: 12.5 cm! Both of us! No more arguments!

Amma points to the ceiling fan. It\'s rotating.

Amma: Speaking of measurement — what about MOTION? See the fan?

Ahana: It\'s spinning — going in circles.

Amma: That\'s circular motion. The fan blades return to the same spot — that also makes it periodic motion.

Swara: Like the clock\'s hands!

Amma: Exactly. What about a swing in the park?

Ahana: Back and forth — oscillatory motion!

Amma: And a train moving on straight tracks?

Swara: Linear motion — moving in a straight line!

Amma: Brilliant. Four kinds of motion: linear, circular, oscillatory, and periodic — sometimes mixed together. The ball rolling on the ground? Linear AND circular at the same time!

🧪 Lab Experiments

Activity 5.1 — Measure with a Ruler Easy

Materials

  • 15-cm scale or metre rod
  • Pencil, comb, eraser, book — to measure

Procedure

  1. Place scale beside the object.
  2. Read length with your eye DIRECTLY above the marking.
  3. Note the unit (cm).
  4. Compare with a friend\'s measurement.

Activity 5.2 — Curved Lines with Thread Easy

Materials

  • Thread or string
  • Scale
  • A curved line on paper (or boundary of a leaf)

Procedure

  1. Lay thread along the curve carefully.
  2. Mark the end of the curve on the thread.
  3. Straighten the thread on the scale.
  4. Read the length.

Activity 5.3 — Spot Different Motions Easy

Procedure

  1. For 10 minutes, sit by a window. Count every kind of motion.
  2. Sort: linear / circular / oscillatory / mixed.
  3. Discuss with classmates.

Add-On 1 — Pendulum Time Medium

Materials

  • String (50 cm)
  • Small weight (washer or nut)
  • Stopwatch (phone)

Procedure

  1. Tie weight to string. Hang from ceiling.
  2. Pull slightly to one side. Release.
  3. Time 10 full oscillations.
  4. Divide by 10 = time period.

Add-On 2 — Body Units Lab Easy

Procedure

  1. Measure the classroom door in handspans, foot lengths, paces.
  2. Each student does the same.
  3. Compare results — are they the same?
  4. Now measure in cm. Now they should agree!
