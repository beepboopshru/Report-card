Diversity in the Living World

Key Concepts

"Trees stand in the Sun and give shade to others. Their fruits are also for others. Likewise, good people bear hardships and bring welfare to others."
— Wise Sanskrit saying
Big Idea

Diversity Means Variety

Look around. In just one school garden, you can find dozens of different plants and animals — each unique.

This variety of living beings in a region is called biodiversity.

Every bird species has its own unique chirp. Every leaf has its own shape. That's biodiversity!
2.1

Plants Are Different

Plants vary in:

  • Height — tiny grass to huge banyan
  • Stem — hard / soft, thick / thin, green / brown
  • Leaves — shapes, sizes, arrangements
  • Flowers — colour, size, scent
Animals

Animals Are Different Too

Animals vary in:

  • Where they live — land, water, air, trees, underground
  • What they eat — plants, meat, both
  • How they move — walk, run, swim, fly, slither
  • Sounds they make, colours, body parts
2.2

Why Group?

So much variety can be confusing. We group things by their common features. This makes them easier to study and remember.

Groupings can be based on:

  • Shape, size, colour
  • Where they live
  • What they eat
  • How they move
2.2.1

Herbs, Shrubs & Trees

TypeHeightStem
🌱 HerbSmallSoft, green, tender
🌿 ShrubMediumHard, woody, many stems from ground
🌳 TreeTallSingle thick hard stem, branches up high

Tomato = herb. Rose = shrub. Mango = tree.

Climbers & Creepers

Weak-Stem Plants

  • Climbers: weak stem, need support to grow UP (money plant, grape vine)
  • Creepers: weak stem, spread along the ground (pumpkin, watermelon)
Both have weak stems — but one goes up, the other crawls along!
Leaves

Venation — Pattern of Veins

  • Reticulate venation — net-like pattern (hibiscus, mango)
  • Parallel venation — veins run side by side (banana, grass)

You can tell a lot about a plant from its leaves alone!

Roots

Tap Root vs Fibrous Root

  • Tap root: ONE main thick root + side branches (mango, gram)
  • Fibrous root: many thin roots from base, no main one (grass, wheat)
Bonus rule: plants with reticulate-vein leaves usually have tap roots. Parallel-vein leaves usually have fibrous roots!
Animal habitat

Grouping Animals by Habitat

HabitatExamples
🌳 TreesMonkey, squirrel, woodpecker
🌍 LandCow, dog, ant, elephant
🐟 WaterFish, octopus, dolphin
✈️ AirBirds, butterflies
🐸 Both land & waterFrog, crocodile
Feeding

Grouping by Diet

  • 🌱 Herbivores — eat only plants (cow, goat, deer)
  • 🥩 Carnivores — eat only other animals (lion, eagle)
  • 🍎 Omnivores — eat both (humans, bears, crows)
Big point

All Connected

Plants and animals are not separate — they depend on each other:

  • Trees give shelter & food to animals
  • Animals spread seeds (after eating fruits)
  • Bees pollinate flowers
  • Earthworms enrich soil for plants
This network = an ecosystem. Every species matters!
India

India's Rich Biodiversity

India has many biodiversity hotspots:

  • Western Ghats — forests, elephants, tigers
  • Himalayas — snow leopards, rhododendrons
  • Sundarbans — Royal Bengal tiger, mangroves
  • Andaman & Nicobar — coral reefs, unique species
Indian Sanskrit tradition has long valued nature — "Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam" (the world is one family).

📚 The 5E Learning Path

The 5E Model — Engage, Explore, Explain, Elaborate, Evaluate — is how scientists actually learn. Walk through Chapter 2 using all five steps.
1

🎯 ENGAGE

Spark curiosity. Notice something interesting.

📖 A Nature Walk in the Park — Swara, Ahana & Amma

Saturday morning. Swara has come over to Ahana's house. Ahana's amma takes both girls to the neighbourhood park.

Amma: Let's just walk slowly today. Notice everything. Plants, animals, sounds, smells. Don't pluck anything — just look!

Swara: (looking at the grass) Amma uncle look — grass is so short. But that mango tree is HUGE. Same family?

Amma: Same kingdom — plants — but very different groups. The little grass is a herb. The mango is a tree. And in between we have shrubs — like that rose bush.

Ahana: What's the difference?

Amma: Herbs are small with soft green stems. Shrubs are medium with many woody stems near the ground. Trees are tall with one thick brown trunk.

Swara: (touching a money plant) What about this one? Its stem is weak — it's climbing on the wall.

Amma: That's called a climber. Weak stem, needs support to go up. (points to a pumpkin patch) That pumpkin plant is a creeper — also weak stem, but spreads along the ground.

Ahana: So plants can be: herbs, shrubs, trees, climbers, creepers. Five types just by stem!

Amma picks up a fallen leaf from a hibiscus, and another from a banana plant. She holds them side by side.

Amma: Look closely. The hibiscus leaf has veins that branch like a net — that's reticulate venation. The banana leaf has veins all running parallel — that's parallel venation.

Swara: Each plant has its own design even in its leaves!

A monkey leaps from a tree. A crow caws nearby. A butterfly sits on a flower.

Ahana: Monkeys live in trees, crows fly through the air, butterflies visit flowers... so animals live in many different places!

Amma: Yes! That\'s called habitat — where an animal lives. Some live on land, some in water, some in trees, some can do BOTH like frogs.

Swara: And cows eat grass, lions eat other animals, crows eat anything! Different diets!

Amma: Right — that gives us three groups: herbivores (plant-eaters), carnivores (meat-eaters), and omnivores (both).

Ahana: (thinking) Humans must be omnivores then!

Amma: Exactly. And all these plants and animals together — that's called biodiversity. The variety of life. Every species has a role in the network.

Swara: (looking up at the trees, smiling) So many lives, all connected!

Activities from the textbook + bonus add-ons. EasyMediumAdult Help

📘 From the Textbook

Activity 2.1 — Nature Walk & Observation Easy

Materials Needed

  • A notebook + pen
  • Water bottle
  • An adult to accompany you
  • A nearby park / garden / forest

Procedure

  1. Walk slowly through the area.
  2. For each plant you see — note stem type, leaves, flowers (if any).
  3. For each animal you see — note where it lives, how it moves, what it eats.
  4. Do NOT disturb plants or animals. Just observe!
  5. Make a table at home with your observations.

What It Teaches

Plants and animals are everywhere — but we have to LOOK to see the variety.

Activity 2.3 — Picture Grouping Game Easy

Materials Needed

  • Old magazines, newspapers, or printouts
  • Scissors, glue, cards (or A4 sheets cut into quarters)
  • Group of 5-6 friends

Procedure

  1. Each person cuts 10 pictures of plants & animals.
  2. Pool all cards in the centre.
  3. Decide a feature to group by — height, colour, habitat, food, etc.
  4. Sort into groups.
  5. Compare with other student-groups. Did they pick the same criteria?

Activity 2.4 — Identify Herbs, Shrubs, Trees Easy

Materials Needed

  • A garden or park
  • Notebook

Procedure

  1. For each plant: measure / estimate height.
  2. Touch the stem — soft & green or hard & brown?
  3. Where do branches start? Close to ground = shrub. Higher up = tree.
  4. Classify each as herb, shrub, or tree.

Activity 2.5 — Leaf Venation Sort Easy

Materials Needed

  • ~10 fallen leaves of different plants
  • Magnifying glass (optional)
  • Notebook

Procedure

  1. Look at the back of each leaf — see the veins.
  2. Reticulate (net-like) → one pile.
  3. Parallel → another pile.
  4. Tape examples in your notebook.

Activity 2.6 — Tap vs Fibrous Roots Medium

Materials Needed

  • An open area with weeds (with permission)
  • A grass plant + a small herb (like radish if available)
  • Water to wash roots

Procedure

  1. Carefully dig up a small grass plant + a small herb.
  2. Wash the roots gently in water.
  3. Compare: grass has many thin similar roots (fibrous). Herb may have a thick main root (tap).
  4. Match with the leaf venation — parallel-vein → fibrous; reticulate → tap.

🎁 Bonus Add-Ons

Add-On 1 — Animal Bingo Easy

Procedure

  1. Make a 5×5 grid with 25 different animals (a mix of land/water/air/tree).
  2. Take it on a walk. Tick each animal as you spot it!
  3. First to fill a row shouts BINGO.

Add-On 2 — Plant a Climber and a Creeper Medium

Procedure

  1. Get money plant cuttings (a climber) + pumpkin seeds (creeper).
  2. Plant the climber near a wall or stick.
  3. Plant the pumpkin in open ground.
  4. Watch them over weeks — climber goes UP, creeper spreads SIDEWAYS.

Add-On 3 — Build a Biodiversity Diary Easy

Procedure

  1. Every day for 30 days, note ONE new plant or animal you see.
  2. Include a small sketch + where you saw it.
  3. At the end of the month — count. You\'ve documented your local biodiversity!
