Exploring Magnets

Big Idea

Magnets — Invisible Pull

A magnet can pull (or push) certain objects WITHOUT touching them. Iron filings dance to it. Pencil boxes click shut. Compass needles point north. All because of MAGNETIC FORCE.

Magnets do not need a battery — they always work!
4.1

Magnetic vs Non-Magnetic

Materials that stick to a magnet = magnetic. Examples: iron, nickel, cobalt (and many alloys of these).

Materials that DON\'T = non-magnetic. Examples: plastic, wood, glass, paper, rubber, aluminium, copper.

Aluminium and copper are metals but are NOT magnetic! Only iron, nickel, cobalt and their alloys are.
Shapes

Magnets Come in Shapes

  • Bar magnet — rectangular
  • Horseshoe (U-shaped)
  • Ring magnet — donut
  • Disc magnet — flat circle
  • Compass needle

Modern magnets in fridges, headphones, motors, MRI machines.

4.2

Two Poles — N and S

Sprinkle iron filings on a bar magnet. They cluster at the two ENDS.

These ends are called POLES:

  • 🟥 North pole (N)
  • 🟦 South pole (S)
⚠️ Even if you break a magnet in half, BOTH halves still have N + S! You can never have a magnet with just one pole.
Rule

Like Repels, Unlike Attracts

Bring two magnets close:

  • N + N → push apart (REPEL)
  • S + S → push apart (REPEL)
  • N + S → pull together (ATTRACT)
"Like" poles repel, "unlike" poles attract. Same rule as in life — opposites attract!
4.3

Magnets Find Direction

Hang a bar magnet by a thread. It SETTLES pointing roughly NORTH-SOUTH.

That\'s because Earth is a giant magnet!

  • One end of the magnet points to Earth\'s north → called the NORTH-seeking pole
  • Other end → SOUTH-seeking pole
Compass

The Magnetic Compass

A small box with a magnetised needle that spins freely. The needle ALWAYS points north-south.

  • Used for centuries by sailors
  • India had its own version: matsya yantra — a magnetised iron fish floating in oil
  • Replaced today by GPS but still essential for hiking, sailing
Earth

🌍 Earth — Giant Magnet

Earth has its own magnetic field. The reason: hot molten iron in its core spinning around.

This field is what makes compasses work. It also protects us from harmful radiation from space!

Animals like birds, bees, even some fish can DETECT Earth\'s magnetic field — they use it to navigate during migration!
Make a magnet

Make an Iron Needle into a Magnet

Rub one end of a bar magnet along an iron needle — in ONE direction only, 30-40 times. Lift the magnet between strokes.

The needle becomes a temporary magnet!

Used to be how compass needles were made before mass production.
Care

Care for Your Magnets

  • 🚫 Don\'t drop them — can lose magnetism
  • 🚫 Don\'t heat them — heat destroys magnetism
  • 🚫 Keep away from electronics (phones, watches, debit cards!)
  • ✓ Store bar magnets in pairs (N to S)
India

🇮🇳 Indian Navigation Heritage

Long before European compasses, Indian sailors used a magnetised iron fish floating in oil — called matsya yantra (fish-instrument).

Indian merchants traded across the Indian Ocean for centuries — and magnetic compasses helped them!

Real-life

Magnets Everywhere

  • 🔊 Speakers + headphones (every one!)
  • 🚪 Refrigerator door (closing mechanism)
  • 🛒 Recycling — magnets separate iron from waste
  • 🏥 MRI machines (giant magnets for medical imaging)
  • 🚝 Maglev trains (float on magnets!)
  • ⚡ Every electric motor

Saturday at Swara\'s house. The girls dig out an old box from her appa\'s shelf. Inside — a tiny circular brass box with a clear glass top. Inside that: a needle balanced on a pin.

Swara: What is THIS?

Ahana: Looks like... a compass?

Swara\'s Appa: It IS a compass! Belonged to my grandfather. Now — turn the box around. Notice anything?

Ahana: The needle always points the same way! Even when I rotate the box!

Appa: Yes — that\'s because the needle is a small magnet. It always points north-south. The red end is the North pole.

Swara: But WHY does it point north?

Appa: Because the Earth itself is a giant magnet! Magnetic poles inside the Earth pull on the compass needle.

Ahana: What sticks to a magnet?

Appa brings a fridge magnet. Tests it on different things.

Appa: Iron objects stick — they are magnetic materials. Plastic, wood, paper don\'t stick — they are non-magnetic. Even most metals like copper and aluminium don\'t stick — only iron, nickel, and cobalt do!

Swara: So that\'s why my pencil case stays closed — the magnet inside is pulling on an iron piece!

Appa pulls out two bar magnets. Brings the red ends together — they push apart!

Ahana: They REPEL each other!

Appa: Yes — like poles repel. Now turn one around. (They attract.) Unlike poles attract!

Swara: What if I break a magnet in half?

Appa: Surprise: each half STILL has both a North and a South pole. You can\'t get a magnet with just ONE pole — ever!

Ahana: (looking at compass) Did people use this before GPS?

Appa: For centuries! Indian sailors had a similar compass called matsya yantra — a magnetised iron fish floating in oil. It pointed north and helped them sail across the Indian Ocean.

🧪 Lab Experiments

Textbook activities + add-ons.

Activity 4.1 — Sort by Magnetism Easy

Materials

  • 1 magnet
  • Various items: pencil, eraser, iron nail, paper clip, aluminium foil, paper, plastic spoon, wooden block, coin

Procedure

  1. Predict which items stick to the magnet.
  2. Test each item.
  3. Record results in a table.
  4. Conclusion: which materials are magnetic?

Activity 4.2 — Iron Filings Pattern Medium

Materials

  • 1 bar magnet
  • White paper
  • Iron filings (from a workshop or grind a rusty nail)

Procedure

  1. Place a bar magnet under a paper.
  2. Sprinkle iron filings on the paper.
  3. Tap the paper gently.
  4. Observe — filings form lines from N to S poles!

What It Teaches

Magnetic FIELD LINES. Strongest at the poles. The lines reveal the invisible force.

Activity 4.3 — Suspended Magnet Test Easy

Procedure

  1. Tie a string to the middle of a bar magnet.
  2. Suspend it freely — let it stop.
  3. It always settles N-S!
  4. Mark the direction on the floor.

Activity 4.4 — Make a Compass Medium

Materials

  • Iron sewing needle
  • Bar magnet
  • Small piece of cork
  • Bowl of water

Procedure

  1. Stroke the needle in ONE direction with one pole of the magnet, 30-40 times.
  2. Test: needle should now attract pins.
  3. Push needle through cork.
  4. Float on water. Watch — it points N-S!

Add-On 1 — Magnet Through Paper Easy

Procedure

  1. Put a paper clip on a table.
  2. Put a piece of paper on it.
  3. Drag a magnet across the paper — clip moves with it!
  4. Try with a book. Then with a thicker book.
  5. How thick can material be before the magnet stops working?

Add-On 2 — Magnetic Treasure Hunt Easy

Procedure

  1. Hide 10 small iron objects in a tray of sand.
  2. Give a magnet to a friend.
  3. Time how fast they find all 10!

Add-On 3 — Identify Magnetic Substances in Food Easy

Procedure

  1. Crush iron-fortified breakfast cereal with water in a zip bag.
  2. Hold a strong magnet to the bag.
  3. Tiny black iron particles will collect under the magnet — iron in your food!
