Living Creatures: Exploring their Characteristics

Key Concepts

"All living beings move, grow, breathe, eat, excrete, respond, reproduce — and one day, die. These 7 characteristics define life."
Big Idea

Living vs Non-Living

A car moves. A pencil doesn\'t. But a car isn\'t alive — and the pencil isn\'t alive either!

What\'s the difference? Living things share 7 essential characteristics:

  • Movement
  • Growth
  • Need food (nutrition)
  • Breathing / Respiration
  • Excretion (waste removal)
  • Response to stimuli
  • Reproduction
A living being eventually dies — that\'s another sign of life!
1️⃣ Movement

All Life Moves

Animals walk, swim, fly, crawl. Plants don\'t move place to place — but they DO show movement:

  • Flowers opening & closing
  • Climbing plants winding around supports
  • Insectivorous plants like Drosera trapping insects with sticky hairs
  • Touch-me-not (chhui-mui, mimosa) folding its leaves when touched
2️⃣ Growth

All Life Grows

You used to fit in baby clothes. Now they\'re too small!

Plants, animals, even tiny seeds — all grow bigger over time.

A car gets bigger only if assembled. A tree grows on its own. That\'s a key difference.
3️⃣ Food

All Life Needs Food

Animals eat plants or other animals. Plants make their own food (via photosynthesis — you\'ll learn this in higher classes).

Food provides ENERGY and BUILDING BLOCKS for growth.

4️⃣ Respiration

All Life Breathes

You breathe in air (oxygen) and breathe out carbon dioxide.

Plants breathe through tiny pores called stomata on their leaves.

Count your breaths after sitting, then after running. Different! Your body adjusts oxygen intake.
5️⃣ Excretion

All Life Excretes

Removing waste:

  • Humans → sweat (water + salts), urine
  • Plants → excess water as drops on leaf tips (early morning dew on grass!)
6️⃣ Stimulus

All Life Responds

Touch a hot cup — you pull back FAST. Step on a thorn — you jump!

Anything that causes a response is a stimulus.

  • Mimosa (chhui-mui) folds leaves at touch
  • Amla tree folds leaves at sunset
  • Plants grow toward light
  • Animals run from danger
7️⃣ Reproduction

All Life Reproduces

Cats have kittens. Dogs have puppies. Trees grow from seeds. Without reproduction, life would end!

A pencil never makes a baby pencil — reproduction is unique to living things.
10.2

Seed Germination

What does a seed need to grow into a plant? Activity 10.2 (4 pots) reveals:

  • ✓ Water — softens seed coat, helps embryo develop
  • ✓ Air — needed for respiration; comes from spaces in soil
  • ✓ Right amount — too much water drowns the seed!
  • For bean seeds, sunlight is NOT essential for germination (but is needed later for growth!)

🌿 Famous Indian Plants

  • Neem — air-cleaning, medicinal

India

Ancient Indian Botany

India has a deep tradition of studying plants — including in Charaka Samhita and Sushruta Samhita (ancient medical texts), and in Ayurveda. Many medicines come from plants:

  • Turmeric — antiseptic
  • Tulsi — immunity
  • Neem — dental health
  • Aloe vera — skin

📖 Swara, Ahana & Amma in the Garden

Saturday morning. Swara and Ahana sit on the lawn with Ahana\'s amma. Around them — birds, ants, plants, and a curious cat.

Swara: Amma uncle, what makes us ALIVE? Like, what\'s the difference between us and a stone?

Amma: Good question! Living beings share seven things. Let me show you.

She points to the cat — it stretches, walks across the lawn.

Amma: One: movement. Animals can move place to place. Plants can\'t walk — but they still show movement. See the touch-me-not?

Ahana touches a mimosa plant\'s leaves. They FOLD up!

Ahana: It moved!

Amma: Yes — that\'s also a response to stimulus. The plant felt your touch, and responded.

Swara: What about growth?

Amma: Two: growth. Look how tall you\'ve gotten! Three: living beings need food — for energy. Four: we all breathe — we call it respiration.

Ahana: But plants don\'t have lungs!

Amma: They have tiny pores on leaves called stomata. Same job — bring oxygen in.

Swara: What\'s five?

Amma: Five: excretion — getting rid of waste. We sweat and urinate. Plants drop excess water as droplets on leaves.

Ahana: And six is responding, you said.

Amma: Right. And seven: reproduction. Cats have kittens. Trees drop seeds that grow into new trees. Without this — no life would continue.

Swara: So a car has wheels and moves... but doesn\'t breathe, grow, or have baby cars!

Amma: Exactly. Non-living. Now — what about seeds? Are they alive?

Ahana: Hmm... they\'re still and don\'t breathe right now.

Amma: They\'re alive but DORMANT — sleeping. Give them water, air, and right conditions — they germinate into plants. We say seeds have life inside, waiting.

🌱 Experiment 1: Seed Germination — 4 Pots

Plant bean seeds in 4 pots: (A) dry+sun, (B) flooded+sun, (C) moist+dark, (D) moist+sun. Wait a week. Which grow? Why?

🌿 Experiment 2: Touch-Me-Not Test

Find a mimosa plant. Touch a leaf gently. Watch it fold! Time how long until it opens again.

📊 Experiment 3: Living-or-Not Survey

List 20 things around you. Sort each as living, non-living, or once-living (paper, leather, milk). Argue your reasoning!

🧪 Lab Experiments

Activity 10.1 — Living vs Non-Living List Easy

Materials

  • Notebook + pen
  • Your eyes!

Procedure

  1. List 15 things around your classroom or home.
  2. For each — write Living or Non-living.
  3. Justify with at least 2 reasons.

Activity 10.2 — 4-Pot Germination Test Easy

Materials

  • 4 small pots + soil
  • 16 bean seeds
  • Watering can
  • 2 weeks of observation

Procedure

  1. Plant 4 seeds in each pot.
  2. Pot A: sun, NO water. Pot B: sun, EXCESS water. Pot C: moist, DARK. Pot D: moist, SUN.
  3. Check daily for 2 weeks.
  4. Conclusion: which conditions give best germination?

Activity 10.3 — Touch the Mimosa Easy

Procedure

  1. Find a chhui-mui (touch-me-not) plant in your garden.
  2. Touch a leaf gently — watch it fold!
  3. Time how long to open again.
  4. Try with light vs strong touch — does the speed change?
